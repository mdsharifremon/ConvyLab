# Home, section 4: How we work

Status: DRAFT copy, waiting for approval before design.

## Job of this section
Take the fear out of starting. A buyer who has been burned by an agency wants to know: what happens after I reach out, will I be buried in jargon, will I be surprised at the end? Section 3 is how the website works on a visitor. This section is how WE work with the client.

## Copy
Eyebrow: How we work
Heading: Simple to start. Clear all the way.

1. **Tell us what's hurting**
   Start with a short form. Tell us the one problem costing your business the most. No long brief, no sales pitch.
2. **We find the real problem**
   We talk, look at your site and ask the questions that separate what truly hurts from what only feels urgent. You get a short plan in plain words.
3. **We design and build**
   One team from first sketch to launch. We pick the platform that fits your business, and you see the work early, so nothing surprises you at the end.
4. **We run it and report**
   After launch we keep your site fast, safe and working. Every month you get a plain-English report on what happened and what we are doing next.

Button: Let's talk

## Why (so later changes are informed)
- Step 1 mirrors the popup form ("Tell us what's hurting."), so the section and the CTA tell one story.
- Step 2 is the discovery (Mom Test style): ask about real problems and past behaviour, not opinions. Written without the method's name.
- Step 3: "we pick the platform that fits" is the only mention of tools. No stack names on the site.
- Step 4: monthly report is written as work we do, never as a result. "Plain-English" answers the jargon fear.
- No time range yet (open item). No prices (custom).

## Rejected
- Naming tools or the discovery method.
- "Free audit" as step 1: dropped, contact only.

## Open items
- Real range of weeks for step 3 (add one line like "Most sites launch in X to Y weeks" only when confirmed).
- Is the monthly report included on every plan?
- Design risk: sections 3 and 4 are both four-step sequences back to back. Decide in design whether to give 4 a different shape.
